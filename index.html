About

A prototype covering 4 of the source book's 30 chapters (Central Personnel Agencies, DHS, Education, DOJ). Built to illuminate, not persuade. Independent — not affiliated with the Heritage Foundation or any campaign, party, or advocacy organization.

Sourcing

Plan text is quoted directly from Mandate for Leadership: The Conservative Promise (2023) with page citations. News items are tagged High / Medium / Low credibility by source type. Verify everything — that's the point.

Status labels

Proposed = no documented action yet. In Progress = formal steps underway. Implemented = enacted or in effect. Blocked / Stalled = attempted but halted (often in court). Statuses reflect linked reporting, not opinion.

How this works

The rule this site lives by

Every card keeps two things strictly separate: What the policy plan says — quoted verbatim from the Mandate for Leadership book with a page citation — and What is happening now — linked, credibility-rated journalism and government records. No blending, no editorializing, no implied narratives.

Status labels

  • Proposed: in the book, but no documented government action tracked yet.
  • In Progress: formal steps underway — executive orders, proposed rules, introduced bills, announced reorganizations.
  • Implemented: enacted, finalized, or in effect.
  • Blocked / Stalled: attempted but halted, typically by courts.

Statuses are assigned from the linked reporting below each card, and every status note says why. If the evidence is mixed, the card says so.

Credibility ratings

Articles are tagged by source type, not by whether their findings are convenient:

HighOfficial documents and wire services — Federal Register, court filings, agency releases, AP, Reuters.
MediumMajor outlets with editorial standards — NYT, WaPo, NPR, Politico, broadcast news, trade press.
LowOpinion pieces, partisan outlets, advocacy organizations — still linked, clearly flagged.

What this is not

Not exhaustive (4 of 30 chapters so far), not predictive, and not a scorecard of good or bad. Absence of a linked report does not mean nothing happened. When the administration acted contrary to a recommendation, the card says that too.
